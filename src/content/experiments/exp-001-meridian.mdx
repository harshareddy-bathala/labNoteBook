## Abstract

Satellite passes are short and they do not repeat. If a station is pointed at the
wrong one, that data is gone. Meridian is open software that decides which passes
are worth receiving, catches them, checks the result, and tells the owner what
happened. It is my final-year project, built with a team of three, and it is
written to outlast the course.

## Problem

Ground stations usually chase the highest pass. Elevation is a weak proxy for
usable data. Element-set age, the station's own history and local interference all
matter, and nobody reports afterwards whether the pass actually worked.

## What I built

- Six modules on FastAPI and PostgreSQL/TimescaleDB: orbit propagation, yield
  prediction, a scheduler, a station registry, observations, and reliability.
- A dashboard that shows why each pass was chosen or skipped, so the schedule is
  never a black box.
- **MSP 0.1**, an open four-endpoint HTTP/JSON protocol that any station can
  implement. It is small enough for a microcontroller and resilient to dropped
  connections and reboots. It is frozen.
- The reliability module, which owns the indicators, the objectives and an
  irrecoverable-loss budget.

## The decision I am proudest of

A pass only counts as missed if the station's heartbeat proves it was listening on
the right target, frequency and time. **Absence is not a miss.** Without that rule a
station outage reads as a reliability failure, and every number downstream is
quietly wrong. It is enforced in exactly one place.

## Where it stands

The software is built and tested: 41 of the 42 conditions in the acceptance document
are proven on every change, and the last is a 72-hour run on the station's own
computer. A practice fleet of 50 simulated stations exercises the whole pipeline,
faults included, and everything it produces is labelled as simulated. Station 001 is
still being built, so no measured result is published. The whole platform has to come
up on a clean machine in under ten minutes, and CI fails the build if it does not.
