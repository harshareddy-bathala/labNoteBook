## Abstract

The plant's body is a graph of tissues. Each one reads its local environment,
exchanges signals with its neighbours and acts: grow, thicken, steer, defend. Reward
comes from survival, biomass and reproduction. The headline controller is
brain-free and decentralised, and its rule is evolved with the cross-entropy method,
with no backpropagation. Neural controllers (a PPO policy and a per-tissue graph
network) are kept as baselines.

## Result

In a multi-seed, statistically tested benchmark the bioelectric controller beats the
neural baselines overall, and by a significant margin under herbivory (+2.83,
p = 0.008), where its electrical wound-to-defence response matters. The learned
signalling shows three signatures of real plant signalling: systemic spread, a slow
finite-speed wave, and a wound response directed along the shoot.

## The finding I would defend

Signalling helps for coordination problems such as defence. It does not help with
plain environmental variability. A paper-style write-up with methods, figures and
results is in the repository, which is private.
