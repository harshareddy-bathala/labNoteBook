I had a good plan for placement prep. Thirteen weeks, sensible order, the right
topics. It failed twice, and the plan was not the reason.

## Why it failed

Every day on the plan had a date. When I missed one, the plan did not shrink to fit
me. It turned red and stayed red. After a few of those the plan is no longer a guide,
it is a record of how far behind you are, and the easiest move is to stop opening it.

There were other gaps too: no reminders, no accountability, no curated resources. But
the calendar was the one that did the damage.

## Index by days closed

Cairn keeps the same roadmap and changes what time means. Progress is indexed by
`day_index`, the count of days on which I actually closed a log. A journey week is
seven active days, not seven calendar days, so every weekly quota corrects itself.

Skipping three days costs a streak. It does not cost the plan. There is no calendar,
and so nothing is ever overdue.

Two small mechanisms keep it honest:

- A catch-up toggle (1x, 1.5x, 2x) lets one strong day absorb two skipped ones.
- A bad-day button collapses the plan to the minimum chain, one problem plus the log,
  so the streak survives a day when nothing else is possible.

## Git is the source of truth

The content has a similar principle. The curriculum lives in the repository as typed
TypeScript and is pushed into Postgres by a seed script. The database is a cache.
Seeding is idempotent, runs in a single transaction, and never deletes on its own,
because progress rows hang off content rows, and deleting a unit would delete
everyone's work on it. A slug that has progress must never be renamed.

## The takeaway

Most planning tools are built around the schedule, and a schedule is the thing you
will break first. Measuring progress in work done, not time elapsed, removes a whole
category of guilt without removing any of the structure.
